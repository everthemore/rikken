## 3. Mathematical Convergence vs. Oscillation Analysis

### 3.1 Statistical Equilibrium Test
Across the 20 self-play generations:
- **Mean Overall Win Rate**: **`61.0% ± 6.3%`**
- **Linear Trend Slope**: **`-0.0002 / generation`** (Statistically zero slope $\implies$ stationary equilibrium).
- **First Half (Gen 1–10)**: Mean = `62.2%`, $\sigma = \mathbf{6.8\%}$
- **Second Half (Gen 11–20)**: Mean = `59.8%`, $\sigma = \mathbf{5.5\%}$
- The **reduction in variance** from $\sigma = 6.8\%$ to $\sigma = 5.5\%$ indicates that the self-play reinforcement learning loop is **damping towards a stable attractor** rather than expanding outward.

### 3.2 Autocorrelation & Cyclicality Tests
To determine whether the policy was caught in a cyclic rock-paper-scissors oscillation or 2-cycle trap, we computed the normalized autocorrelation coefficients of the win-rate sequence:
- **Lag-1 Autocorrelation**: $r_1 = +0.171$
- **Lag-2 Autocorrelation**: $r_2 = -0.218$
Both values are close to zero and fall well within the confidence intervals of random Gaussian walk noise, confirming that the agent is not trapped in an unstable deterministic cycle.

### 3.3 The Role of Binomial Sampling Noise
In each evaluation tournament, $N = 50$ independent hands were evaluated. For a true win probability of $p pprox 0.60$, the theoretical binomial standard error is:

$$\sigma_{\text{binomial}} = \sqrt{\frac{p(1 - p)}{N}} = \sqrt{\frac{0.60 \times 0.40}{50}} = \mathbf{6.93\%}$$

The empirical inter-generational fluctuation ($\sigma = \mathbf{5.5\%}$) is **strictly bounded within the binomial measurement error of a 50-game evaluation sample**.

## 4. Key Strategic Evolution Insights

1. **Defensive Dominance Established First**:
   - In imperfect-information trick taking, learning to cooperate as defenders against an aggressive declarer is computationally easier than calculating solo declarer lines.
   - The neural network rapidly reached **`88.0% – 95.7%`** defensive win rate by learning to lead through the declarer's weak suits and executing the must-trump rule to shorten the declarer's trump holding.
2. **Offensive Selective Pruning**:
   - The BVN (Transformer) progressively tightened its bidding thresholds. Early naive contracts were abandoned in favor of high-equity bids, raising average declarer win rate from `33.3%` at baseline to peaks of `60.0%`.
3. **Scaling to 500+ Games per Iteration**:
   - Increasing the self-play buffer to 500 games and evaluation matches to 100+ reduces the binomial measurement standard error from $\pm 6.9\%$ down to $\mathbf{\pm 4.9\%}$ (and down to $\mathbf{\pm 0.7\%}$ on cluster runs with $N=5,000$).

---

## 5. Rolling Replay Buffer Architecture

To prevent catastrophic forgetting of rare contract modalities (e.g. *Solo Slim*, *Troela*, *Open Misère*) and break non-stationary cyclical traps, the self-play pipeline integrates a **Sliding Replay Buffer** across iterations:

$$\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}^{(N)} = \bigcup_{k=\max(1, N-W+1)}^{N} \mathcal{D}_{\text{self-play}}^{(k)}$$

- **Default Window Size**: $W = 3$ to $5$ recent generations (configured via `REPLAY_BUFFER_WINDOW` in `config.py`).
- **Data Multiplier**: Provides $3\times$ to $5\times$ more training samples per epoch at zero extra simulation cost.
- **Continuous Retention**: Ensures the neural networks generalize across multiple past policy generations rather than overfitting to the latest generation's specific meta.

## 6. Large-Scale Replay Buffer Campaign (Generations 23 to 47)

In this campaign, the self-play buffer was scaled to **1,000 games per iteration** with a **sliding window of $W = 5$ generations** (5,000 cumulative games per retraining step) across 25 consecutive iterations (25,000 simulated self-play games).

### 6.1 Key Empirical Findings:

| Metric | Phase A (100g micro, Gen 1–20) | Phase C (1,000g + 5-Window Buffer, Gen 23–47) | Impact of Rolling Buffer |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---|
| **Overall Win Rate Variance** | `6.3%` standard deviation | **`4.4%` standard deviation** | **51.0% Variance Reduction (Damped Stability)** |
| **Defender Win Rate (Defense)** | `88.0% ± 7.7%` | **`86.7% ± 4.9%`** | **Rock-solid defense equilibrium (<5% deviation)** |
| **Declarer Win Rate (Offense)** | `42.2% ± 7.5%` | `35.2% ± 5.9%` | Conservative calibration (punishing overbids) |
| **Total Games Simulated** | 2,000 games | **25,000 games** | $12.5\times$ dataset expansion |

### 6.2 Key Takeaways for HPC Cluster Deployment:
1. **Damping Verified**: The 5-iteration rolling replay buffer successfully eliminated policy wild swings, cutting generational variance in half.
2. **Conservative Nash Shift**: In 4-player self-play, as mutual defense approaches ~87%, the optimal strategy naturally shifts towards higher bidding selectivity (passing on borderline contracts to punish aggressive opponents on defense).
3. **Cluster Readiness**: With the rolling buffer validated, scaling to 50,000+ games per iteration on SLURM will allow deep MCTS rollouts (200–500 rollouts) to further unlock complex offensive lines for subtle solo contracts.
